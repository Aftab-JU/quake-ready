03 / WHEN THE GROUND SHAKES

Know your next move.

Follow official local instructions. These reminders are a starting point, not a substitute for emergency services.

01
IF YOU ARE INDOORS

Drop. Cover. Hold on.

  • Drop to your hands and knees before the shaking knocks you down.
  • Cover your head and neck under sturdy furniture if available.
  • Hold on until shaking stops. Stay away from windows and don't rush outside during shaking.
REMEMBER

Protect your head and neck. If there is no sturdy cover nearby, get low beside an interior wall away from glass.

05 / BEYOND YOUR HOME

Safer neighbourhoods start with a conversation.

Engineering can help communities prepare, but readiness also depends on neighbours knowing how to help one another. Start small and make the plan inclusive.

Plan a neighbourhood check-in →
01

Find the people who may need help

With their consent, plan how to check on older adults, children, people with disabilities, and anyone who may need assistance evacuating.

02

Walk the common areas

Notice blocked exits, unsecured objects, and confusing routes. Report building concerns to the owner or responsible authority.

03

Practise together

Arrange a short preparedness discussion or drill with your housing society, school, or local group. Follow guidance from local authorities.

Do not enter damaged buildings or attempt structural repairs yourself. For suspected structural damage, seek assessment from a qualified professional.
